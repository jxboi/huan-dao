# 03 · Licence, Rental & Riding Rules

## Licence
* Foreign visitors need an **International Driving Permit (IDP) with the motorcycle (A) category** + their home licence + passport. Reputable shops will not rent without it (they are legally not supposed to).
* Taiwan recognises IDPs under the 1949 Geneva convention for countries with reciprocity. **⚠ VERIFY** per nationality (e.g., some countries such as Japan use a translated licence instead).
* An IDP is typically valid for **30 days after entry**; stays longer need a local licence conversion.
* Residents (ARC holders) need a Taiwanese licence after the grace period.
* Rural shops are sometimes more relaxed; big-city shops (esp. Taichung) may insist on a Taiwanese licence. Riding without a valid licence voids insurance — the app must say so plainly.

## Rental
| Vehicle | Typical price / day (2025–26) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 125cc scooter (e.g. Kymco, SYM, Yamaha) | NT$400–900 low season; up to NT$1,200–1,800 peak | The standard Huan Dao ride |
| 150–155cc scooter (SMAX, Force, Jet) | NT$700–1,200 | Better on mountain roads, 2-up |
| Gogoro / electric (battery swap) | NT$500–900 | ~100 km real range per battery set; GoStations everywhere in west/north, sparser on the east coast |
| Heavy bike 250–550cc+ | NT$2,000–4,000 | Heavy-bike licence endorsement needed |

* **Multi-day discounts** are common (e.g., 10–30 % off for a week+). Ask for a Huan Dao package.
* **Deposit**: cash or card hold (NT$3,000–10,000). Avoid leaving your passport.
* **One-way rentals** exist but cost extra (NT$1,000–3,000+). Most riders do a loop back to the pick-up shop.
* Taiwan Railway **stopped shipping scooters as luggage** (2019). Private scooter shipping (機車托運) exists — NT$600–2,000 depending on distance — handy if you need to skip a section (e.g., Suhua in bad weather).
* Check before leaving: tyres, brakes, lights, horn; photograph existing scratches; get the shop's phone number and roadside-help process; confirm insurance (compulsory third-party only by default — ask about extra).
* Helmets are provided; bring your own liner/buff. Rain gear (雨衣) is sold at every 7-Eleven.

## Key riding rules
* Ride on the **right**. Scooters use the right-most lane / scooter lane.
* **Two-stage left turn (兩段式左轉)** at signed intersections: go straight to the waiting box (painted white box) on the far right corner, turn to face left, wait for green. Mandatory for white/green plates where signed; heavy bikes turn like cars.
* **No right turn on red.** Fine NT$600–1,800.
* **Running a red light:** NT$1,800–5,400.
* **Yield to pedestrians at crosswalks:** up to NT$6,000 (heavily enforced since 2023).
* **Helmet:** mandatory, rider and passenger, NT$500 fine each.
* **Drink-driving:** zero-tolerance levels, huge fines (NT$30,000+), licence revocation.
* Speed cameras are everywhere; many are fixed poles, some mobile. Typical limits: city 40–50, provincial highway 50–70 km/h.
* Headlights on in tunnels. Many tunnels on Suhua/South Link are long.
* Parking: park in painted scooter bays (some are paid — slip on the seat, pay at 7-Eleven). Do not park on sidewalks unless marked.

## Fuel
* 92 / 95 / 98 unleaded. Most scooters use 92 or 95 (ask shop). Attendant fills the tank — say "jiā mǎn, jiǔ-wǔ" (加滿 95).
* Pay cash, card, or EasyCard at most CPC stations. Stations are 24h in cities, often close ~20:00–22:00 in rural east.
